Childhood

The kitchen on
Willow Street

It always began with the kettle. The blue one, with the small chip on the handle, set on the back burner before the rain had even stopped.

Mom would be at the table with flour on her sleeves, rolling dough in slow, certain circles. The radio sat on the sill and sang a little too loudly. I would sit on the counter and watch the window fog up, one small circle at a time.

It is strange, the things we keep. Not the whole kitchen — just the smell of cinnamon, and the sound of rain against the glass.
