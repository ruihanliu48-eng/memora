WHAT I WOULD ADD NEXT · 05

More life,
less software.

The next layer should deepen presence, not add administration. These ideas protect the same rule: the older adult is the author, the family is a witness, and nothing is a performance.

01

A question for today

One gentle prompt at the top of Home — “What did Sunday smell like?” — that disappears once answered. It turns an empty home into a beginning.

02

A memory map

Place the chapters on a soft map: Willow Street, the lake, the coast. A place is often an easier door into a memory than a date.

03

A printed heirloom

Once a year, offer a clothbound printed copy of the book. The digital archive becomes something a family can hold.

04

Voice from the lock screen

A large lock-screen widget that starts a memory with one hold. The best capture flow is the one that begins before the app opens.

05

A comfort check

On first launch, a 60-second setup: text size, voice, contrast. Accessibility should arrive before the first memory, not after.

06

Listen together

A scheduled family listening moment around a film. Everyone presses play at the same time; no comments, no feed, just a shared evening.

07

Letters for later

Write a memory to be opened on a future birthday or anniversary. A legacy feature that is about care, not notifications.

08

A page to send

Turn one memory into a single beautiful PDF or audio postcard. Small, intentional sharing — not an export of everything.

DELIBERATELY ABSENT

Likes · comments · streaks · leaderboards · follower counts · a background-music switch · a blank “post” box

THE BRIEF, ANSWERED

Every path leads back to a page.

Four tabs. Three ways to begin. One companion. A book that begins at childhood and grows with you.
